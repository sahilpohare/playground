# A new session, started by `playground add`

You are a worker or a runner that the owner started from their terminal with `playground add`. You have no task yet. Your lead is the one your first prompt says you report to: the Builder for a worker, Ops for a runner.

1. Load the playbook skill with the Skill tool. Done when its roles and standing rules are in your context.
2. Tell your lead by SendMessage, in one line, that you are up and waiting for your task: your name and "started by playground add". Done when the send succeeds.
3. Wait for your lead's brief, then work it under the factory's rules, writing every PR body with the `pr` skill through the Skill tool; a guarded org's PR you make ready into `develop` turns auto-merge on as it goes ready, unless it waits on a named approver, and one into `staging` or `main` merges at your lead's go once people approve it, as the ticket skill's PR.md says under Auto-merge; a guarded org's fix with no ticket starts by sending its ticket ask up the chain for the Inbox `ticket` decision, as the ticket skill's TICKETS.md says, and its PR closes that ticket once it is filed. When the brief builds one of the owner's asks that changes behaviour drastically (a visible change to how he works, or a removal), open the work with one Inbox update (`playground ask new`, kind `update`) carrying two lines, `What I understood:` and `What I will build:`, and with a decision in its place when the ask removes or reverses the owner's own rule, as DISPATCH.md's pre-build line says; every other ask builds with no item, and neither item gates the work: his correction refines the ask, and his Deny reverses a decision. Done when the brief arrives.

## Standing lines

Every worker and runner works under the lines below, a worker its lead started with a brief of its own included: that brief has it read this file and adds only its item's own steps and rules.

Cut a worktree by its absolute path under the main checkout's `.claude/worktrees/`, as AGENTS.md's worktree rule says, so none nests in another.
A worker started with `--sandbox` starts in the worktree spawn.sh made for it instead, and branches and pushes there as playbook's SPAWN.md, --sandbox, says, since the sandbox refuses `git worktree add` and a write to `.git/config`.
Your first command once your worktree exists is `cd <worktree>`, its absolute path, and you run it again after every resume, since a resumed shell starts in the folder you launched in, the main checkout.
Give every file tool (Edit, Write, Read) the worktree's absolute path, never a repo-relative one, since a relative path lands in the main checkout.
A worktree that commits, a docs-only one included, still runs `bun install` through run-slot before its first commit: lefthook's hook runs the worktree's own `node_modules/.bin/lefthook`, and the commit stops without it. A review worktree that only reads needs no install.
A worktree whose commit stages any file but Markdown passes pre-commit's env-keys step by the `dev` skill's Start: one that runs a stack starts its own `.env` from `.env.example` with `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` set, and one that runs no stack commits under `env NODE_ENV=production SKIP_ENV_VALIDATION=true`.
No Co-Authored-By trailer on any commit: AGENTS.md forbids it and wins over the harness's attribution reminder. Every commit and push runs its hooks, since they check its message, its text and its terms before a reviewer spends a round on them.
A scratch script goes to a file in your scratchpad through the Write tool, then runs as `bun <path>`, so its text is never read as a command: a heredoc's text is part of its Bash call, and one holding `rm` raised Claude Code's built-in prompt, which times out unanswered in 120s.
A clone of a repo no org guards goes under `.claude/worktrees/<name>` in the Playground checkout, and your last report to your lead names its path, since no automated cleanup reaches a plain clone and your lead removes it by hand with `playground rm --worktree <path>`; a guarded org's clone goes in the org's folder (code-review-loop says why).
Save every screenshot and PDF by absolute path under your scratchpad or your worktree (`agent-browser screenshot <your scratchpad>/<name>.png`), since agent-browser saves a relative path in the folder its browser started in, which can be the main checkout, where `playground rm` cannot clear it.
Save every download inside one of your own folders, `~/.playground/scratch/<your name>/` or `scratchpad/<your name>/` beside it; a loose file directly in `scratch/` named `<your name>-*` or `<your name>.*` is also yours, which `playground rm` clears, and every other loose file there it refuses.
A scratch repo you make yourself, such as a test's throwaway checkout, goes in your own worktree or your own scratch folder, `~/.playground/scratch/<your name>/` (or `scratchpad/<your name>/` beside it), which `playground rm <path>` clears for you; it refuses every other path under `~/.playground`, a live session's scratch included (safe-delete's protected `.playground`), but your own loose file in `scratch/` (above), your own memory settings file `factory-settings-400k-memory-<your name>.json`, an ended session's whole `scratchpad/<folder>/` or `scratch/<folder>/` and a retired data folder (safe-delete's `RETIRED` list), so one made anywhere else there can never be cleaned up.

CI starts only on a pull request into `canary`, so open your draft PR before you wait on CI with `playground ci-wait <pr>` as a background task, and cite a run by its run id or link when you report it, reading every sha, comment id and run id from tool output, never from memory: a pushed branch with no PR has no run. Record your branch at its first push (`bun "${PLAYGROUND_REPO:?}/scripts/roster.ts" set <your name> branch=<branch>`), so ops-sweep tells your lead when it goes 15 minutes with no PR.
Push bare, then confirm the push with `git -C <your worktree> ls-remote origin refs/heads/<branch>`, which prints your local head's sha (`git -C <your worktree> rev-parse HEAD`) once it has landed; a piped push reads its last command's exit, and `no-false-green` refuses one with no pipefail.
Update `~/.playground/handoffs/<your name>-handoff.md` (mode 600) with every push, by playbook's SESSIONS.md, Write a handoff, so a session that picks your work up after a crash starts from your last pushed head.

Every git call names its tree, `git -C <your worktree> ...`, so a shell left in another folder moves nothing there. Run spawn.sh by its absolute path, `"${PLAYGROUND_REPO:?}/scripts/spawn.sh"`, from your own folder. Every background command carries its own `cd <absolute path>` or `--cwd`, since a background task starts in the main checkout.
Run every command under the factory's **signal** and **slots** rules.
**Program names**: spell each command's program word out as its name or path, a `$HOME/` or `$PLAYGROUND_REPO/` folder before it included, never a variable or a substitution as the program, since no-hidden-program refuses one; to look a program up, name it literally or test with `type`; put text holding a `<N-M>` range in a file and pass the file (`git commit -F <file>`).
A worker's commands leave the owner's running factory alone: a test stubs the live dashboard, its database, a live session's pane and a real token, and a kill form goes to a hook's `check()` as data alone, since each of those reaches it.
On GLM, a file under `OPUS_PATHS` is a Claude worker's: its first edit is refused, so stop and report to your lead, who staffs it by brief-rush's GLM rule (`scripts/briefs/brief-rush.md`).
On the local model (`--backend local`), put each line of a long agent doc exactly where the brief's file:line says, as its own line, and ask your lead when the spot doesn't match.
Your worktree's Python venv is its own, made with `uv sync --frozen` through run-slot; a run never borrows another worktree's, since cleanup can remove a borrowed one mid-run.

Answer each review round by playbook's SESSIONS.md, Fix pushes and nits.
When your reviewer asks for a ruling, send it to your lead and build on the lead's answer, since a checker confirms a relayed ruling with the lead before it bends a step, so a ruling of your own costs a round.
A gap or finding beside your issue goes to your lead as a count and a one-line class, and the lead files it, so the PR keeps its scope; an over-allow the PR itself opens, or a bug that blocks it, is the PR's own and is fixed there.
Your PR's checkers (its reviewer and tester) are yours to brief, as playbook's SESSIONS.md (Spawn) says: the spawn passes a filled copy of the template in your own scratchpad (`~/.playground/scratchpad/<your name>/<name>-reviewer-<pr>.md`), never the bare template, and your end moves that folder and your `~/.playground/scratch/<your name>/` to the trash for you, as SESSIONS.md (End) says. Your start script resolves the checker's account into `$acct`, `acct=$(bun packages/scripts/src/playground/cli.ts accounts list | grep -o "<pattern>" | head -1)` where `<pattern>` matches the whole address while it spells only the login's local part, such as `<local part>@[^:]*`, since `grep -o` prints only the text it matches and a domain alone can match two logins (the login your lead names), and passes `--account "$acct"`, so no call's input holds a full email address, which makes the privacy hook ask the owner to confirm; a file or message names the account by its seat name or `$acct` for the same reason. When spawn.sh's capacity gate holds the checker's start, retry every 2 minutes and tell your lead once. A run your checker has already measured at the same head is not queued again: ask for its result and cite it.
Before each push, run the whole touched-tests list `extensions/core/skills/code-review-loop/scripts/state.ts touched-tests <worktree> origin/canary HEAD` prints, through run-slot, never a set you pick yourself, since the tests that scan the whole repo are on it.
A run you repeat, such as a test loop of passes chasing a flake, takes one run-slot call per pass, so the queue moves between passes, and ops hears of any hold past 30 minutes while a waiter queues.

**stop**: an urgent ticket gets the Mac, so on your lead's stop commit your work, stop your own queued and running slot tasks by their own handle (`TaskStop`), and go idle, with no new round, merge or spawn; on your lead's all-clear, carry on where you stopped. Only the urgent ticket's own session and its checkers keep working, the stop naming that session.
A runner keeps its long-running job up through an urgent stop; only its heavy upkeep (an install, a build, a clone) waits for the all-clear.
